Shopify vs WordPress: which is better for a small business?

Both platforms power millions of websites, but they're built for different jobs. Here's how to pick the right one for what your business actually needs.

By Umme Ruman, Founder & CEOOctober 30, 2026
The short answer

If your main goal is selling products online and you want something easy to run, choose Shopify. If your website is mainly about services, content or a blog, or you want full control, WordPress is usually the better fit.

The short difference

Shopify is an all-in-one online store platform. Hosting, security, payments and updates are handled for you, and you pay a monthly subscription.

WordPress (the self-hosted version from WordPress.org) is free, open-source software that powers a huge share of the web. You choose your own hosting, theme and plugins, and you can sell online by adding a plugin such as WooCommerce.

In simple terms: Shopify is like renting a fully furnished shop. WordPress is like owning the building and furnishing it yourself.

When Shopify is the better choice

  • You sell physical or digital products and want to start quickly.
  • You don't want to think about hosting, security updates or backups.
  • You need reliable checkout, inventory, shipping and payment tools in one place.
  • You plan to sell on Instagram, Facebook or TikTok as well as your website.

When WordPress is the better choice

  • You offer services (a clinic, a consultancy, a studio) and need a site that turns visitors into enquiries.
  • Content and SEO are central to your plan, for example a blog or resource library.
  • You want full control over design, features and where your data lives.
  • You sell a small number of products alongside lots of content.

What about SEO?

Both platforms can rank well on Google. What matters far more is the quality of your content, how fast your pages load, how well the site works on mobile and how clearly each page is structured. WordPress gives you more control over technical SEO and blogging, while Shopify covers the basics well for product pages.

Hidden costs to plan for

  • Shopify: paid apps add up quickly, and using a payment provider other than Shopify Payments can add extra transaction fees.
  • WordPress: premium plugins, a good hosting plan, and someone to handle updates and fixes if you don't want to.
  • Both: a premium theme or a custom design, and your domain name.

A simple way to decide

Ask yourself one question: "Will most of my revenue come from people buying products directly on my website?"

If yes, start with Shopify. If no, and your website's job is to build trust, explain your services and bring in enquiries, start with WordPress or a custom-built site.

Frequently asked questions

Is Shopify better than WordPress for beginners?

For online stores, yes. Shopify handles hosting, security and checkout for you, so beginners can launch faster with fewer technical decisions.

Is WordPress really free?

The WordPress software is free, but you still pay for hosting, your domain, and often premium themes or plugins.

Can I move from WordPress to Shopify later?

Yes. Products, customers and content can be migrated, though it takes planning to keep your URLs and Google rankings intact.

Which is better for SEO, Shopify or WordPress?

Both can rank well. WordPress offers more control and stronger blogging tools, while Shopify covers the essentials for product pages. Content quality and site speed matter more than the platform.
