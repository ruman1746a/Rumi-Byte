How much does a logo design cost in 2026?

From free logo makers to five-figure agencies, prices are all over the place. Here is what a logo really costs, what drives the price, and what you should get for your money.

By Umme Ruman, Founder & CEOOctober 30, 2026
The short answer

A professional logo for a small business usually costs somewhere between $150 and $2,500. Logo makers and marketplaces sit below that, branding agencies far above it. For most new businesses, an independent designer or small studio gives the best balance of quality and price.

Quick answer: what a logo costs in 2026

There is no single price for a logo, because "a logo" can mean anything from a template with your name on it to a fully researched brand identity. These are the rough ranges you will see in the market today:

OptionTypical costWhat you usually getBest for
Logo makers and AI toolsFree to $50A template-based mark, limited file typesTesting an idea before you invest
Freelance marketplaces$50 to $500Quality and originality vary a lotTight budgets with time to review carefully
Independent designers and small studios$150 to $2,500Research, original concepts, revisions and a full file setMost small and growing businesses
Branding agencies$5,000 and upStrategy, naming, large identity systemsEstablished, well-funded companies

These are market ranges, not fixed prices. They change with the designer's experience, the country they work from and, most of all, the scope of the project.

What actually affects the price

Two logos can look equally simple and still have very different prices. The difference is usually in the work you don't see:

  • Research and strategy. Understanding your audience, competitors and positioning before sketching anything.
  • Number of concepts and revisions. One idea with two rounds of changes costs less than three directions with unlimited tweaks.
  • What's delivered. A single logo file is very different from a logo suite, colour palette, fonts and brand guidelines.
  • Ownership. A professional designer transfers full rights to you. Cheap templates often can't be trademarked because other businesses use the same icon.
  • Experience and timeline. Senior designers and rush deadlines both cost more.

A logo is not the same as a brand identity

A logo is one mark. A brand identity is the whole system around it: your colours, typography, how photos and graphics look, and the rules that keep everything consistent on your website, social media and packaging.

If you only buy a logo, you will still have to decide all of these things later, usually one post or one flyer at a time. That's how brands end up looking slightly different everywhere. Paying a little more for a basic identity at the start often saves money and confusion later.

What files you should receive

Whatever you pay, make sure you walk away with everything you need to use your logo anywhere. A complete handover includes:

  • Vector files (SVG, PDF or EPS) that can be resized for a business card or a billboard without losing quality.
  • PNG files with transparent backgrounds for websites and social media.
  • Logo variations: a main logo, a simpler version and an icon for small spaces like profile pictures.
  • Colour versions: full colour, black and white.
  • Colour codes (HEX, RGB and CMYK) and the font names.
  • Written confirmation that you own the design.

Red flags with very cheap logos

A low price isn't always a problem, but watch out for these signs:

  • The design uses a stock icon or template you can find elsewhere.
  • You only receive a JPG, with no vector or transparent files.
  • No questions are asked about your business before the design starts.
  • There's no mention of who owns the final design.

How much should you budget?

A simple rule of thumb that works for most businesses:

  • Just starting out: budget roughly $150 to $400 for a professional logo with the core files.
  • Growing business: budget roughly $400 to $1,500 for a logo plus colours, fonts, guidelines and social templates.
  • Rebrand of an established company: expect more, because research, rollout and testing take time.

Whatever you choose, think of your logo as something you'll use every single day for years. Spread over that time, a good one costs very little.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a professional logo take?

Most small business logos take one to three weeks, depending on research, the number of concepts and how quickly feedback comes back.

Can I use an AI-generated logo for my business?

You can, but AI and template logos are often hard to trademark and may look similar to other brands. They work for testing an idea, but a custom logo is safer for a business you plan to grow.

Do I own my logo after paying a designer?

You should. Ask for written confirmation that full rights are transferred to you, along with the original vector files.

Is a logo enough, or do I need full branding?

A logo is a good start. Adding a colour palette, fonts and simple guidelines keeps your website, social media and print materials consistent, which makes your business look more trustworthy.
